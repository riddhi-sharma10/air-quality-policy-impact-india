## 4. Findings (none changes a number; items 1 to 4 are needed before sign-off)
1. **Medium. "Cluster by station only" (CI +10.1 to +13.6) is too narrow and should not be shown as a robustness check.** Fire counts vary by date and are shared by all stations, so only the date-clustered interval is credible. F9 shows it without a warning. Relabel it "not used: ignores correlation between stations on the same day" or move it to an appendix. Related: the residuals are strongly autocorrelated (daily ACF lag 1 = 0.54, significant to about lag 7, F10), which clustering by date does not remove. The Newey-West cross-check is wider (+23.1%, CI -0.2 to +51.8, p = 0.053). I would quote both and say the primary interval is probably somewhat too narrow.
2. **Medium. Two of the 15 robustness checks are empty by construction.** "Drop near-real-time fire rows" and "Drop zero-assumed fire days" return the primary result exactly, because the winter sample contains neither (near-real-time starts 2 Jul 2026; zero-assumed days fall in June to September). They inflate "14 of 15 same sign": the informative count is **12 of 13**. Say so in T07a and F9.
3. **Medium. K3 does not answer RQ1 as written.** Climatology (RMSE 28.5 µg/m³) and persistence (25.3) beat both regression models (weather only 32.3, weather + fire 32.1). The test months January and February are winter, but the training period (19 Feb to 31 Dec 2025) holds no January and only 19 to 28 February, so the seasonal terms extrapolate (January RMSE about 71 against about 50 for the baselines, F11). The 0.6% RMSE gain from fire compares two weak models. For RQ1 I suggest the in-sample figure from section 2: **fire adds 0.6 percentage points of R2 (partial R2 1.5%) after weather, calendar and station effects in the winter sample**. Keep K3 as an exploratory result and say plainly that the model is not a useful forecast.
4. **Medium. F10 fails the figure checklist** (labels overlap between panels, fractional lag ticks); see section 5.
5. **Low. Specification count.** The K2 docstring says 4 specifications; the code runs 6 (A, B, C1, C2, C3, D) plus Newey-West, and K4 adds 15 checks. Two of the five alternative fire measures have a CI that excludes 0 (FRP, +4.3%, 0.2 to 8.5; 200 km count, +6.6%, 3.3 to 10.0) while the primary does not. Report all of them; do not pick.
6. **Low. Specification D is mislabelled.** `n_nh_100km_lag2` is the all-direction count summed over the previous two days (it includes the lag-1 day), not "the fires of the day before".
7. **Low. K1 lag profile is flat:** r = 0.35, 0.37, 0.30, 0.32, 0.33, 0.37 for lags 0 to 5, including the lag-0 diagnostic. That pattern reflects the shared autumn rise of fires and PM2.5, not a lag-specific fire effect. Say so next to F8.
8. **Low. Use a transformed or capped rain term. The effect on RMSE is small but the cluster at y = 0 is visible.
9. **Info. Placebo with next-day fires:** +3.0% (CI -9.4 to +17.1). Its interval contains the primary estimate, so the placebo does not clearly separate the result from slow-moving confounding. Mention it.
10. **Info. F7 shows the largest fire counts in April to May 2025 and 2026** (wheat residue, up to about 300 a day) and only about 45 a day in the Oct to Nov 2025 season. The winter sample has a weak fire signal (mean 4.1 upwind detections), which fits the wide interval.
