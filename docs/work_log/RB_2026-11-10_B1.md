5. Choice: **B as the primary set, A and C descriptive.** Frozen rule (written before looking at outcomes):
   - **Episode:** consecutive changes 24 hours apart or less are merged (correction made before running: the 16 and 17 Jan 2026 changes are exactly 24 hours apart, 21:00 to 21:00, so "less than 24" would have missed them; this is a structural fix, no outcome was seen). Stage before = stage before the first change, stage after = stage after the last, time = the first change.
   - **Primary event:** an episode whose net direction is up and whose stage after is 2 or higher, and that has (i) no other stage change in the 7 days before it, (ii) no other change in the 3 days after the last change of the episode (the effect window days 1 to 3), (iii) no network outage hour from 14 days before to 7 days after, (iv) at least 40 valid days of PM2.5 in the 60 days before (a day is valid with at least 18 valid hours at at least half of the treated stations).
   - **Relaxed set (sensitivity):** same but (i) uses 3 days instead of 7.
   - Day 0 (the calendar date of the change, IST) is never used for fitting or for the effect, because the hour of the change is uncertain. Effect windows are days 1 to 3 and days 4 to 7 after the date of the last change of the episode.
5b. **Amendment A1 (made after the first run, before looking at any PM2.5 outcome around an event).** The frozen condition (iii) "no outage hour from 14 days before to 7 days after" left **0 primary events**: the 928 outage hours are scattered in many short gaps (1 to 85 hours inside nearly every window), so almost every window contains some. Condition (iii) was meant to stop missing data biasing the result, and the valid-day rules (iv and "all 3 post days valid") already do that. So (iii) is **replaced by a flag** (`outage_hours_window` is kept in the table, and the effect days must be valid) and is no longer an exclusion. The relaxed set now also accepts 2 of the 3 post days valid. The first-run result (0 events) is kept in this note so the change is visible. The amendment was prompted by the exclusion table (dates and counts of outage hours), not by pollution levels.
