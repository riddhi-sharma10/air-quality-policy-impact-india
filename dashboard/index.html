Burning, GRAP and Delhi-NCR air quality

54 stations · hourly · 19 Feb 2025 – 30 Sep 2026 · associations, not proven effects
The question

Do crop-residue fires and emergency restrictions (GRAP) change PM2.5 in Delhi-NCR once the weather is accounted for?

We joined hourly air-quality readings from 54 stations with satellite fire detections, weather and the restriction stage in force. This page shows what the data can and cannot tell us.

i
Start here. Two hypotheses were tested with rules written down before the models were fitted. H1: more fires upwind the day before goes with higher PM2.5. H2: PM2.5 falls after a GRAP stage is switched on, compared with what weather and fires predict.

The link is real in the data, but it shrinks to +1.7% when days are compared within the same date: much of it is simply "fires and smog are both high in November". 4 of 9 ways of computing the interval include zero.

H2 · GRAP activation
Inconclusive

Primary event, for information only: -21.7% (95% interval -39.8% to +1.3%). It fails the hold-out rule.

Too few events gave a usable prediction, so no verdict is possible. This reflects too little data, not evidence for or against GRAP.

What we found
1
Delhi-NCR is far dirtier than the control cities. Winter PM2.5 averaged 163 µg/m³ against 65, and 95% of winter days exceeded the 60 µg/m³ standard.
2
Fires help explain PM2.5, but weather matters far more. Adding fire data to the model improves prediction only slightly.
3
We cannot yet say whether GRAP works. It is switched on when pollution is already high, so a later fall is expected anyway, and there are too few events.

H1 · Fires and PM2.5

Do days after more upwind fires have higher PM2.5?

"Upwind fire" = a satellite-detected fire within 100 km of a station, in the direction the wind came from, the day before. The model compares the same station across days, holding wind, temperature, humidity and boundary-layer height fixed.

How big is the association between upwind fires and PM2.5 once weather is controlled?
About +9.5% per +5 upwind detections (about +15 µg/m³ at the winter mean).
How sure?

the interval is +1.4% to +18.3% and it falls from +9.5% to +5.5% with month effects. Caveat: it is mostly a between-month association and one winter.

How much do fires add beyond weather?
Little: 1.8 points of R-squared on top of 45% from stations and weather, and +0.91 µg/m³ (about 2%) of test error.
How sure?

the gain interval is +0.37 to +1.40, but it is -0.09 once yesterday's PM2.5 is in both models (negative: slightly worse). Caveat: the test period has no burning season.

Does the fire link survive seasonal controls?

Each row is the same estimate under a stricter comparison. Green = the interval excludes zero (clear association); grey = it includes zero (cannot rule out no link). Dot = estimate, line = 95% interval.

Read down the rows: once the model compares days within the same month, week or date, most of the apparent fire effect disappears. Fire adds only 1.8 points of R² beyond weather and station differences.
H2 · GRAP and PM2.5

Does PM2.5 fall after a GRAP stage is switched on?

GRAP stages (1 to 4) add stricter restrictions as air worsens. For each activation we asked: was PM2.5 in the next three days lower than a model of weather and fires would have predicted? That prediction is called the counterfactual.

After activation, is PM2.5 lower than predicted?
The frozen model says -21.7% for the primary event, but it fails the hold-out rule (error 90%).
How sure?

not at all; at most 1 of 4 fitted events pass the hold-out rule under any alternative, and the control-city comparison shows +9.7%. Caveat: no usable counterfactual and very few events.

Is the fall just mean reversion?
It cannot be told apart: the primary event changed by +6.6% against a median of -9.8% on comparable days, and the excess beyond mean reversion is +43.8% (interval -15.5% to +142.9%).
How sure?

the intervals include zero. Caveat: stages are invoked on high and rising days, so a fall after activation is expected even without a stage.

Bars show the smallest true fall the method would reliably (80% of the time) detect, for a given number of events.

Data
  • PM2.5: CPCB via OpenAQ, 54 stations, 763,344 station-hours
  • Fires: NASA FIRMS (VIIRS)
  • Weather: ERA5
  • Restrictions: CAQM GRAP orders
  • 928 outage hours; longest run 242 hours (Jan 2026), treated as missing
  • 971 hours at Stage III or IV
Limits
  • One burning season and one winter
  • GRAP starts when pollution is already high
  • Fires counted within 100 km; cloud gaps are not zeros
  • Control cities are a comparison, not a clean counterfactual
  • No Punjab or Haryana stations
Glossary
PM2.5
Fine particles under 2.5 µm that reach deep into the lungs; measured in µg/m³.
GRAP
Graded Response Action Plan: emergency restrictions in stages 1–4 across the NCR.
Treated / control
Stations inside the NCR (GRAP applies) vs stations in Patna, Lucknow, Kanpur.
Upwind fires
Fires within 100 km in the direction the wind came from, the previous day.
Counterfactual
Our estimate of PM2.5 without the stage, from weather and fires.
Confidence interval
The range the true value plausibly lies in; if it includes zero, "no effect" is possible.
